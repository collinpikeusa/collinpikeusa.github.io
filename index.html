About the operator

Ham radio, the 21st-century way

Licensed as KJ4AXB, now WE4RR — using the hobby the way it works best for me: weak-signal digital, in the field, with a small footprint.

I got into amateur radio for the same reason a lot of people do — the idea that a modest antenna and a few watts can put you in touch with the other side of the planet. What kept me in it was discovering how well that works when you pair a classic HF rig with modern weak-signal software.

In 2022 alone I logged upwards of six thousand FT8 contacts on the FT-897D and the Alpha Antenna MagLoop, and passed ten thousand contacts inside a single year. None of it from a tower or a beam — just a loop on a tripod and a lot of patience.

These days the centre of gravity is Parks on the Air. I started activating in the Mid-Atlantic — Maryland, DC, Delaware, West Virginia — and have since moved south to Augusta, Georgia, where Mistletoe State Park has become my home park. The gear packs into one weatherproof case, sets up in about ten minutes, and runs all afternoon off a lithium power station.

I also had the good fortune to operate as KJ4AXB/VP9 from Bermuda. Everything I work goes to Logbook of the World, and I answer every card that comes my way.

The station

One case, ten minutes, on the air

The whole portable setup — radio, antenna, power, and interface — travels in a single weatherproof case.

Transceiver

Yaesu FT-897D

HF through UHF in one box, happy on internal batteries or external DC. The workhorse for every activation since day one.

Antenna

Alpha Antenna MagLoop

10–40 m portable magnetic loop rated to 100 W. Tripod-mounted, no radials, no trees required — which is exactly what a park picnic area calls for.

Power

Jackery Explorer 300

Lithium power station that comfortably outlasts a full afternoon of digital operating without a generator or an idling engine.

Digital interface

SignaLink USB & DigiRig Mobile

SignaLink at home, the much smaller DigiRig Mobile when pack space matters. Both handle audio and PTT over a single USB run.

Computer

Lenovo ThinkPad T440p

Rugged, repairable, and cheap enough that a rainstorm at a picnic table is not a catastrophe. Runs the whole digital stack.

Transport

Apache 4800 case

Weatherproof protective case holding the rig, interface, and cabling. Grab it, and the station is packed.

Field notes on the loop: The MagLoop will take 50 W of FT8 happily across most of its range. Down low the ALC starts to complain, and on 80 m the smaller loop realistically limits you to 10–20 W. It is a fair trade for an antenna that deploys in minutes and needs no supports at all.
